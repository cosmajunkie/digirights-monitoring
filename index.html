    India · Digital Rights

    Know your rights.

    Your digital rights are constitutional rights. Here is what you are entitled to — and what to do when those rights are violated.

    In 2017, a nine-judge Supreme Court bench unanimously held in K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India that privacy is a fundamental right under Article 21. Art. 21

    This extends to your digital communications, data, and online activity. Surveillance without lawful authority and targeted monitoring without due process can be challenged as violations of this right.

    Article 19(1)(a) protects your right to freedom of speech and expression online — your posts, messages, and journalism are covered. Art. 19(1)(a)

    Section 66A of the IT Act, used to arrest people for online posts, was struck down in Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015) as unconstitutional. Restrictions must be proportionate and within the narrow grounds of Article 19(2).

    In Anuradha Bhasin v. Union of India (2020), the Supreme Court held that freedom to carry on a trade or profession over the internet is protected, and shutdowns must be necessary, proportionate, and subject to judicial review. Art. 19(1)(g)

    Any shutdown order must be published and can be challenged in a High Court or the Supreme Court. Document the time, location, and scope — this is exactly what Sākṣhī is designed for.

    The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 gives you rights over personal data held by organisations in India: access, correction, erasure, and the right to grieve through a Data Protection Board. DPDPA 2023

    Under IT Rules 2021, major platforms must acknowledge grievances within 24 hours and resolve them within 15 days. IT Rules 2021

    Steps: (1) Screenshot the removal notice. (2) File a formal grievance via the platform. (3) Contact the platform's India Grievance Officer if unresolved. (4) Approach the Appellate Committee under the Rules. Document everything.

    You have the right to be informed of the grounds for arrest immediately, to consult a lawyer of your choice, and to be produced before a magistrate within 24 hours. Art. 22

    Do not surrender devices without legal advice. Contact a lawyer immediately. Reach out to: Internet Freedom Foundation, SFLC.in, Lawyers Collective, or Human Rights Law Network.

    Use a reputable VPN for encrypted traffic. Use Signal for sensitive conversations. Enable two-factor authentication on all accounts. Archive content at archive.org before it is removed.

    At a protest: consider disabling biometric unlock — a fingerprint or face can be compelled in ways a PIN cannot. Clear your browser history after using this site if surveillance is a concern.
