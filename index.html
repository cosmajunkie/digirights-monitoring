    India · Digital Rights

    Know your rights.

    Your digital rights are constitutional rights. Here is what you are entitled to — and what to do when those rights are violated.

    In 2017, a nine-judge bench of the Supreme Court unanimously held in K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India that privacy is a fundamental right protected under Article 21 of the Constitution — the right to life and personal liberty. Art. 21

    This extends to your digital communications, data, and online activity. Surveillance without lawful authority, mass data collection, and targeted monitoring of citizens without due process can all be challenged as violations of this right.

    Article 19(1)(a) of the Constitution guarantees every citizen the right to freedom of speech and expression. The Supreme Court has confirmed this right extends to digital and online expression — your posts, messages, and journalism online are protected. Art. 19(1)(a)

    Section 66A of the IT Act, used to arrest people for online posts, was struck down in Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015) as unconstitutional. Restrictions on expression must be proportionate and within the narrow grounds listed in Article 19(2).

    In Anuradha Bhasin v. Union of India (2020), the Supreme Court held that the freedom to carry on any trade or profession over the internet is protected, and any shutdown must be (a) necessary, (b) proportionate, and (c) subject to judicial review. Art. 19(1)(g)

    Any shutdown order must be published and can be challenged before a High Court or the Supreme Court. If you witness a shutdown, document the exact time, location, and scope — this is exactly what Sākṣhī is designed for.

    The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 gives you rights over personal data held by organisations operating in India: the right to information about your data; the right to correction and erasure; the right to nominate a representative; and the right to grieve through a Data Protection Board. DPDPA 2023

    If a platform removes your content or suspends your account and you believe this involved processing of your personal data, you may raise a grievance under this Act in addition to platform-level mechanisms.

    Under the IT (Intermediary Guidelines) Rules 2021, significant social media intermediaries must publish a Grievance Officer in India, acknowledge your complaint within 24 hours, and resolve it within 15 days. IT Rules 2021

    Steps: (1) Screenshot the removal notice immediately. (2) File a formal grievance via the platform's own mechanism. (3) If unresolved, contact the platform's India Grievance Officer (they must publish this). (4) You can approach the Appellate Committee under the Rules. Document everything — that is where Sākṣhī comes in.

    You have the right to be informed of the grounds for arrest immediately, the right to consult and be defended by a legal practitioner of your choice, and the right to be produced before a magistrate within 24 hours. Art. 22

    Do not hand over your devices without legal advice. Contact a lawyer immediately. Organisations that can help: Internet Freedom Foundation (IFF), SFLC.in, Lawyers Collective, and Human Rights Law Network. Inform a trusted contact of your situation as quickly as possible.

    Use a reputable VPN — it encrypts your internet traffic and masks your location. It can also help you access services during throttling. Use Signal for sensitive conversations — end-to-end encrypted and minimal metadata. Enable two-factor authentication on all accounts.

    Archive before posting. Use archive.org or archive.ph to save copies of important content before it can be removed. At a protest: consider disabling biometric unlock — fingerprint and face can be compelled in ways a PIN or passphrase cannot. Clear your browser history after using this site if surveillance is a concern.
